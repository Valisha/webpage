Welcome to the July Edition of the Boston Women in Bioinformatics Newsletter!

Summer is bringing plenty of reasons to get together, spark new ideas, and celebrate our incredible community! Whether you're looking to expand your network, hear inspiring career stories, catch up on the latest in computational biology, or simply enjoy time with fellow bioinformaticians, this month's newsletter is packed with opportunities to connect, learn, and have fun along the way.

Finally, we're looking back at our recent **Lightning Talks with Boston Computational Biology and Bioinformatics**, where members shared innovative research, sparked thoughtful discussions, and strengthened the collaborative spirit that makes this community so special.

We hope this edition inspires you to get involved, discover something new, and connect with fellow members. Thank you for being an important part of Boston Women in Bioinformatics—we look forward to seeing you at an upcoming event!

## Don't Lose Us in Spam

<div id="avoid-spam"></div>

To ensure you never miss an update, add **newsletter@boston-wib.org** and **communications@boston-wib.org** to your email contacts.

For guaranteed delivery, click below to see specific steps for your provider:

<details class="image-description">
<summary>Instructions</summary>

### Gmail (Desktop)

1. Click **Settings (Gear icon)** > **See all settings**.
2. Go to **Filters and Blocked Addresses** > **Create a new filter**.
3. In the **From** field, paste: `newsletter@boston-wib.org OR communications@boston-wib.org`
4. Click **Create filter**, check **Never send it to Spam**, and click **Create filter** again.

### Outlook (Web)

1. Click **Settings (Gear icon)** > **Mail** > **Junk email**.
2. Under **Safe senders and domains**, click **Add**.
3. Type `boston-wib.org` and press **Enter**.
4. Click **Save**.

### Apple Mail (iPhone/Mac)

1. Open the **Contacts** app and click **+** to create a new contact.
2. Name it **Boston WIB**.
3. Add both email addresses to the contact card and save.

</details>

_Tip: If an email is already in your Spam folder, open it and select **Not Spam** or drag it to your Primary inbox to train your provider._

As scientists, we often describe bioinformatics as objective. But every dataset, every pipeline, and every model is shaped by human decisions. For much of history, those decisions have been primarily driven by male perspectives. At our Fall Fundraiser, female senior leaders in our field will lead us in answering a powerful question: **If the female perspective had been fully represented throughout the history of biomedical science, what would we know today that we don’t know now?**

Women's health is one of the fastest-growing sectors in biotech, and the science driving that growth runs on data. This fall, Boston Women in Bioinformatics is gathering to celebrate what our community is building and to connect the people building it. Join us on September 29th for a panel discussion over dinner, networking, and a raffle with researchers, industry scientists, and data professionals who are shaping what comes next.

This is one of our rare ticketed events. Ticket sales directly fund the community programming and resources we provide year-round. Want to help make it happen? We're looking for volunteers! Reach out to Sammy or Liyang on Slack, or email events@boston-wib.org.

### BWIB x BCBB Lightning Talks, Sponsored by Seqera

_[Dana Moochnick](https://www.linkedin.com/in/dana-moochnick)_ • June 29, 2026

<figure>
  <img
    src="/photos/2026/seqera_lightningtalks_2026_speakers.jpeg"
    alt="Speakers and organizers of the BWIB x BCBB Lightning Talks 2026 sponsored by Seqera"
  />
  <figcaption>Speakers and organizers of the BWIB x BCBB Lightning Talks 2026, sponsored by Seqera</figcaption>
</figure>

Boston Women in Bioinformatics and [Boston Computational Biology & Bioinformatics](https://www.meetup.com/boston-computational-biology-and-bioinformatics-meetup) (BCBB) came together for an inspiring evening of Lightning Talks at the Cambridge Innovation Center. Sponsored by [Seqera](https://seqera.io/), the event created a welcoming space for researchers, students, engineers, and bioinformatics professionals to share their work in a fast, approachable format. Each speaker had just five minutes, but the talks opened up much larger conversations about how computational biology is shaping research across immunology, aging, cancer, genomics, and human health.

What made the evening especially exciting was how naturally the talks moved between scientific discovery and practical problem-solving. Some speakers shared new ways to understand disease biology and treatment response, while others focused on the tools, pipelines, and data practices that make research more reliable and reproducible. Together, the presentations showed the full range of what bioinformatics can be: a field that connects biology, computation, software, medicine, and community.

The event also highlighted the value of bringing people together in person. The Lightning Talk format made the room feel energetic and accessible, giving speakers a chance to practice communicating their work while giving the audience a quick glimpse into many different areas of research. It was a motivating reminder of the creativity, collaboration, and momentum within the Boston bioinformatics community, and of how much can be learned when people are given space to share their ideas.

Read more [here](https://boston-wib.org/blog/quicktake/lightning-talks-2026-reflection?utm_source=newsletter&utm_medium=email&utm_campaign=lightning-talks-2026).

**_Advances in the Development of Therapeutics and Diagnostics for Women's Health_**  
Pharmaceutical & Bioscience Society (PBSS) in collaboration with Women in Bio | Virtual | Jul 22, 11:30 AM–5:30 PM  
Join the Pharmaceutical & Bioscience Society (PBSS) and Women in Bio for a free full-day webcast on the latest advances in therapeutics and diagnostics for women's health. **_The Semantic Layer for Life-Science AI: Building Agents and Apps on the Amass API_**  
Bioinformatics and AI XChange | Virtual | Aug 22, 10:00 AM  
Join Bioinformatics & AI XChange for a hands-on, demonstration-focused session with Alexander Junge, co-founder and CTO at Amass. Junge will introduce Amass, a unified API integrating biomedical publications, clinical trials, regulatory documents, therapeutic molecules, and gene targets. The session covers how to build life-science AI agents that access structured, cross-linked biomedical knowledge through a single API.
